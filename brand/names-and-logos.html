Name and logo exploration

Eight working names and six logo styles for a notes-first D&D companion. None use Wizards of the Coast marks or setting names. The d20 is the one shared symbol, because it is the single shape every table recognises on sight. Everything here is a starting point for you to redraw in Affinity.

Name candidates

Inkhoard

A dragon's hoard, but of notes. Says "collect and guard everything" and fits maps, art and lore. Strong, short, ownable.

Lorewell

A well you draw lore from. Calm and reads well in a sidebar. Less tabletop-specific.

Scribehold

Where the scribe keeps things. Plain meaning: it holds notes. Slightly clunky to say.

Tomekeep

Keep for your tomes. Very fantasy, very clear. Risks sounding like a generic RPG tool.

Longrest

The rest between sessions. A phrase every player knows, and it frames the app as what you do between games. Likely contested as a name.

Hearthscroll

Tavern warmth plus a scroll. Friendly and cosy, a bit long for an app icon label.

Rollbook

Rolls and a book. Literal and easy, covers dice and notes together. Also a school attendance term.

Wyrmledger

A dragon's account book. Memorable and bold, with a harder edge. Hard to spell on first hearing.

I can't check trademarks or domains from here. Before you fall for one, search the name plus "app" and "D&D", and check the .app and .com domains.

Logo styles (shown with a placeholder wordmark)

A

Plain d20. The most direct signal. Needs a twist (an open face, a flame, a page fold) or it looks like every dice app.

B

d20 with a quill. Dice plus writing, which is exactly the product. The quill crossing the die keeps it from being a dice-roller logo.

C

Wax seal. Reads as "sealed letter, campaign record". Great as an app icon and as a stamp on exports.

D

Tome with d20. Most literal "notes plus D&D". Warm and obvious, risks looking like a hundred other RPG apps.

E

Crest. Heraldic, formal, scales well to a favicon. Feels like a guild or a character's coat of arms, which suits character art.

Inkhoard
F

Wordmark first. Letters carry the brand and a small d20 stands in for a letter or sits beside it. Easiest to draw cleanly in Affinity.
